What is an automatic cat feeder with a camera?

It is a regular automatic feeder (a food tank on top, a bowl below, and a motor that dispenses kibble) with a built-in camera pointed at the bowl area. You connect it to your home WiFi and use a phone app to:

  • Set meal times and portion sizes
  • Feed a meal on demand
  • Watch a live video of your cat
  • Talk to your cat through a speaker and hear them through a microphone (2-way audio)

How to choose an automatic cat feeder with a camera

1. Video quality

1080P HD is a good standard. It is clear enough to see whether your cat is eating and how much food is left in the bowl. Also check the viewing angle so the camera actually shows the bowl and the area around it.

2. 2-way audio

With 2-way audio you can call your cat to dinner and hear them meow back. Many owners say this is the feature they use most. It can also help your cat link your voice with mealtime.

3. App and WiFi

The app is where you will spend your time, so it should be simple: easy scheduling, clear portion settings, and a quick live view. Check which WiFi band the feeder supports. Many smart pet devices connect to 2.4GHz WiFi only, so make sure your router offers it.

4. Night view, alerts, and recording

Some camera feeders offer night view, motion alerts, or saved video clips. Some of these features need a paid subscription. Read the product details carefully so you know what is included and what costs extra.

5. Tank size

A 3L tank is a common size for one cat and means fewer refills. How long it lasts depends on your cat's portions and the food you use.

6. Number of bowls

Most camera feeders have one bowl. If you have two cats, look at a dual bowl feeder instead, or use one camera feeder per cat. Our guide to the best automatic cat feeder setup for two cats explains both options.

7. Power backup

Check whether the feeder can run on batteries during a power outage, and whether the camera works on battery power. Often the feeding keeps working while WiFi features stop until power returns.

8. Cleaning

Kibble oils build up on the bowl and inside the tank. A stainless steel bowl and parts that come apart without tools make cleaning easy. Keep the camera lens clean too, so the picture stays clear.

How much does a camera feeder cost?

Prices vary a lot depending on the camera, tank size, and brand. Camera feeders usually cost more than basic timed feeders because you are getting a camera, a speaker, and a microphone too.

Also count the ongoing costs. Some brands charge a monthly fee for cloud video or extra features. Check what works without a subscription before you buy.

Which automatic pet feeder is most reliable?

The most reliable feeder is one that keeps feeding on a saved schedule if WiFi drops, accepts backup batteries, sends app alerts and uses a dispenser that suits your kibble. People often ask which feeders are most reliable. Whatever brand you choose, these habits make a big difference:

  • Use kibble that fits. Very large or oddly shaped pieces can jam the dispenser.
  • Keep the tank dry and the lid closed. Damp kibble sticks together.
  • Check the WiFi signal where the feeder sits. Move it closer to the router if the live view keeps dropping.
  • Use backup batteries if your model supports them.
  • Run a test meal after every refill and watch it on the live view.
  • Turn on app notifications so you know when meals are served.
  • Have a backup person who can check in if you are away for several days.

How do you keep food fresh in an automatic feeder?

Fill only what your cat will eat in a reasonable time, keep the lid closed and the feeder out of the sun, and clean and dry the tank each time it runs empty.

  • Fill the tank with an amount your cat will finish in a reasonable time, instead of topping it up forever.
  • Empty old kibble before adding a new bag, so old food does not sit at the bottom.
  • Keep the lid closed and the feeder out of direct sun and away from heaters.
  • Clean and dry the tank each time it runs empty.

Can you put wet food in a camera feeder?

No, not in the food tank. Automatic feeders with a food tank, including camera feeders, are made for dry kibble. Wet food can spoil at room temperature and can jam the dispenser. If your cat eats wet food, many owners use the automatic feeder for dry meals and serve wet food by hand when they are home.

How to set up a camera feeder

  1. Place it on a flat, stable surface near an outlet and within good WiFi range.
  2. Point the camera so you can see the bowl and the space where your cat stands.
  3. Download the app and connect the feeder to your 2.4GHz WiFi (if required).
  4. Fill the tank with dry food that fits the dispenser.
  5. Set your schedule and portion sizes. Ask your vet if you are unsure how much to feed.
  6. Run a test meal and watch it on the live view.
  7. Introduce your cat slowly. Some cats are surprised by the motor sound at first. Calling them with 2-way audio can help.

Should you talk to your cat through the camera?

Many owners do, and many cats respond calmly. Some cats look around for you or seem a bit confused at first. A few tips:

  • Keep it short, calm and friendly.
  • Use the same phrase at every meal, like "Dinner time!"
  • Watch your cat's reaction. If they hide or seem stressed, keep it to mealtime calls only, or stop.
  • Do not use it to scold your cat. Keep the speaker linked with good things.

How to clean a camera feeder, including the lens

  1. Unplug the feeder.
  2. Empty the bowl and wash it in warm, soapy water.
  3. Wipe the food tank with a damp cloth, or wash removable parts as your manual describes.
  4. Wipe the camera lens with a soft, dry microfiber cloth. For smudges, use a cloth that is barely damp.
  5. Never spray liquid on the camera, speaker or microphone.
  6. Dry everything completely before you refill.

Wash the bowl every few days, the tank every couple of weeks, and the lens whenever the picture looks blurry.

Common camera feeder mistakes

  • Placing it where WiFi is weak. The live view will keep dropping.
  • Pointing the camera badly, so you cannot see the bowl.
  • Relying only on the app for long trips, with no one to check in.
  • Skipping a test meal after setup or a refill.
  • Putting wet food in the tank.
  • Not reading the subscription details before you buy.
  • Forgetting to clean the lens.

Do automatic cat feeders with cameras need WiFi?

Yes, camera feeders need WiFi for the live video, app control and alerts. Many feeders keep feeding on the saved schedule if WiFi drops, but you cannot watch or change meals until it reconnects. Check the product details for how your model behaves offline.

What WiFi do automatic cat feeders need?

Most smart pet feeders connect to 2.4GHz WiFi, and many do not support 5GHz networks. Check that your router offers a 2.4GHz band before you buy. Place the feeder where the signal is strong so the live view does not keep dropping.

Do camera feeders need a subscription?

Live view usually works without a subscription, but some brands charge for extras like cloud video storage, recorded clips or advanced alerts. Always check what is included for free before you buy. Count any monthly fee as part of the real cost of the feeder.

Can I talk to my cat through the feeder?

Yes, you can talk to your cat through a feeder that has 2-way audio. You speak through the phone app, and you can hear your cat through the feeder's microphone. Many owners use it to call their cat to meals.

Should I talk to my cat through the camera?

You can, and many cats respond calmly to a familiar voice, especially at mealtimes. Keep it short and friendly and use the same phrase each time. If your cat hides or seems stressed, limit it to mealtime calls or stop.

Is a camera feeder good for two cats?

A camera feeder suits one cat best, because most camera feeders have only one bowl. For two cats, a dual bowl feeder or two separate feeders usually works better. Some owners use one camera feeder per cat so they can watch each one eat.

Can I use wet food in a camera feeder?

No, feeders with a food tank are made for dry kibble only. Wet food can spoil at room temperature and can jam the dispenser. Serve wet food by hand when you are home and use the feeder for dry meals.

Does dry cat food go bad in an automatic feeder?

Yes, dry cat food can go stale or spoil, especially once the bag is open and the food is exposed to air, heat and moisture. Fill the feeder with only what your cat will eat in a few days, keep the lid closed and empty old kibble before adding a new bag. Check the best-by date on the bag and the storage advice from the food brand.

Do camera feeders work during a power outage?

Many camera feeders keep dispensing meals on backup batteries during a power outage, but the camera and WiFi features often stop until power returns. Not every model supports batteries, so check the product details. Put fresh batteries in before you travel if your feeder uses them.
