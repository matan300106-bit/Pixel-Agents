Kittens grow fast. In their first year, they need extra calories, protein, and nutrients to build strong bodies. That is why kitten food exists. But at some point, your kitten becomes an adult cat, and its needs change. This guide explains when to stop feeding kitten food, how to switch without an upset stomach, and how many meals a kitten or young cat should eat.

When to stop feeding kitten food

For most cats, the time to stop feeding kitten food is around 12 months of age. By then, most cats are close to their adult size and no longer need the extra calories of a growth food.

This is a general guideline, not a strict rule. Some things can change the timing:

  • Breed and size. Large breeds, such as Maine Coons, Ragdolls, and Norwegian Forest Cats, often keep growing past one year. Your vet may suggest staying on kitten food longer, sometimes up to around 18 months or more.
  • Spay or neuter. After this surgery, many cats need fewer calories. If your kitten is gaining weight fast after being fixed, your vet may suggest cutting portions or switching earlier.
  • Body condition. A kitten that is getting chubby may need a change sooner. A thin or small kitten may need more time.
  • Health. Kittens with health problems may need a special diet. Always follow your vet's advice.

Why kitten food is different from adult cat food

Kitten food is made for growth, so it usually has more calories, protein, and fat per cup than adult food. It also has nutrient levels set for a growing body.

That is great for a kitten. But an adult cat eating kitten food long term may get more calories than it needs, which can lead to weight gain. Many indoor adult cats are not very active, so extra calories add up fast.

Tip: Some foods say "for all life stages." These are made to meet the needs of growing kittens, so they can be fed from kitten to adult. You still need to adjust the amount as your cat grows. Your vet can help you choose.

Signs your kitten is ready for adult cat food

Your kitten is likely ready for adult food when it is about a year old, is close to its adult size, and has a healthy body shape. Here are some signs:

  • Your cat is around 10 to 12 months old (later for large breeds).
  • Its growth has slowed. Weight changes month to month are small.
  • It has been spayed or neutered and is starting to gain extra weight.
  • Your vet says it is a good time at a checkup.

If you are not sure, ask at your kitten's next vet visit. It is a quick question, and your vet knows your cat.

How to switch from kitten food to adult cat food

Switch slowly over about 7 to 10 days by mixing a little more adult food into the kitten food each day. A sudden change can cause vomiting, diarrhea, or a cat that refuses to eat.

If your cat has a sensitive stomach, stretch each step to 3 days, so the full switch takes about 10 days or more. Going slower is always fine.

Step-by-step tips

  1. Pick the new food first. Choose an adult food labeled "complete and balanced" for adult maintenance.
  2. Check the calories. Adult food may have fewer calories per cup, so the amount may change. Use the feeding guide on the bag.
  3. Mix well. Stir the two foods together so your cat cannot pick out only the old kibble.
  4. Watch the litter box. Soft stool for a day can happen. If it lasts, slow down.
  5. Do not rush a picky cat. If your cat stops eating, go back a step and call your vet if it skips meals. Cats should not go long without food.

How often should a kitten eat?

Young kittens need 3 to 4 small meals a day because their stomachs are small and they grow fast. As they get older, you can slowly move to 2 or 3 meals a day.

The total amount per day comes from the food label and your vet. Divide that daily amount by the number of meals. More meals should not mean more food. For adult portions, see our guide on how much a cat should eat.

Should you free feed a kitten?

Some owners leave food out for very young kittens, and some vets are fine with this for a short time. But free feeding makes it hard to know how much your kitten eats, and the habit can lead to weight gain as your kitten becomes an adult.

Many owners find it easier to start scheduled meals early. Measured meals at set times help your cat learn a routine, make the switch to adult food smoother, and let you notice quickly if your cat stops eating.

Common mistakes when switching to adult food

The most common mistakes are switching too fast, keeping the same portion size, and forgetting about treats. Here is what to watch for:

  • Switching overnight. Even a food your cat loves can upset its stomach if it changes all at once. Follow the 7–10 day chart above.
  • Using the same scoop size. Adult food often has a different number of calories per cup than kitten food. Check the new label and adjust.
  • Not counting treats. Small extras add up fast in a small body. Many vets suggest keeping treats to about 10% of daily calories.
  • Switching during a stressful week. A move, a new pet, or a vet surgery is not the best time. Wait until things are calm, if you can.
  • Forgetting to weigh. Weigh your young cat once a month for the first few months on adult food, and ask your vet if the weight goes up or down quickly.

Frequently asked questions

When should I stop feeding my cat kitten food?

Most cats can switch from kitten food to adult food at about 12 months old. Large breeds may need kitten food a bit longer, sometimes up to around 18 months. Ask your vet for the best timing for your cat.

Can I switch my kitten to adult food at 6 months?

Most kittens are still growing at 6 months and do best on kitten food until about a year old. Some vets may suggest an earlier change, for example if a kitten is gaining too much weight after being spayed or neutered. Do not switch early without talking to your vet.

Is it bad to feed an adult cat kitten food?

Kitten food is not harmful to an adult cat in small amounts, but it usually has more calories than an adult needs. Eaten long term, it can lead to weight gain. Most adult cats should eat a food made for adult maintenance.

What happens if a kitten eats adult cat food?

A few bites of adult cat food will not hurt a kitten. But adult food may not give a growing kitten enough calories and nutrients if it becomes the main diet. Feed kittens a food labeled for growth or for all life stages.

How long should the switch from kitten to adult food take?

A slow switch over 7 to 10 days works well for most cats. Start with about 25% adult food mixed with 75% kitten food, then increase the adult food every 2 to 3 days. Go slower if your cat has a sensitive stomach.

When should Maine Coons stop eating kitten food?

Maine Coons and other large breeds grow for longer than most cats, often well past their first birthday. Many vets suggest keeping them on kitten food or an all-life-stages food longer, sometimes up to 18 months or more. Your vet can check growth and tell you when to switch.

Should I switch to adult food after spaying or neutering?

Spaying or neutering often lowers a cat's energy needs, so some cats start to gain weight after surgery. Your vet may suggest reducing portions or switching to adult food a little earlier. Watch your cat's weight and body shape and ask at the follow-up visit.

How many times a day should a kitten eat?

Kittens around 2 to 3 months old usually need about 4 small meals a day. From 3 to 6 months, 3 to 4 meals is common, and from 6 to 12 months, 2 to 3 meals. Divide the daily amount on the food label by the number of meals.

How many times a day should an adult cat eat?

Most adult cats should eat at least 2 meals a day, about 8 to 12 hours apart. Many cats do well with 3 or 4 smaller meals made from the same daily amount. Smaller meals can help cats that beg or eat too fast.

Can kittens eat dry food?

Yes, most kittens can eat dry kitten food once they are weaned, often around 8 weeks. Some owners mix in wet food or add a little water at first to soften it. Always keep fresh water available, especially if your kitten eats mostly dry food.

Should I free feed my kitten?

Some vets allow free feeding for very young kittens, but scheduled meals make it easier to track how much your kitten eats. Scheduled meals also help prevent weight gain later. Ask your vet what is best for your kitten's age and size.

Can I use an automatic feeder for a kitten?

Yes, an automatic feeder can serve small, regular meals of dry kitten food, which suits a kitten's need for frequent meals. Check that the kibble size flows well and watch the first few meals to make sure your kitten eats. PETME2 feeders let you set meal times and portion sizes in an app and are made for dry food only.

Is "all life stages" food OK for kittens?

Food labeled for all life stages is made to meet the needs of growing kittens, so it can be fed to kittens and adults. You still need to adjust the amount as your cat grows and gets older. Ask your vet if it is a good fit for your cat.

How do I know if my kitten is eating enough?

A healthy kitten should gain weight steadily, have good energy, and eat its meals with interest. Weighing your kitten every week or two is a simple way to track growth. Call your vet if your kitten stops eating, loses weight, or seems tired.
