How much water should a cat drink a day?

Cats need water every day to stay healthy. Water helps with digestion, body temperature, and keeping the kidneys and bladder working well.

The most common general guideline is about 3.5 to 4.5 oz (about 100 to 130 ml) of water per 5 lb of body weight per day. This is the total from all sources: the water bowl, the fountain, and the moisture in food.

Here is what that looks like by weight:

Cat weight Total water per day (about) Roughly
5 lb 3.5 to 4.5 oz about 1/2 cup
8 lb 5.5 to 7 oz about 3/4 cup
10 lb 7 to 9 oz about 1 cup
12 lb 8.5 to 11 oz about 1 to 1⅓ cups
15 lb 10.5 to 13.5 oz about 1⅓ to 1⅔ cups

General guideline for healthy adult cats. Hot weather, activity, illness, and diet can all change how much water your cat needs. Your vet can tell you what is right for your cat.

Wet food vs dry food: why it matters

Wet (canned) food is mostly water, often around 75% to 80%. Dry food (kibble) is usually only around 10% water. This makes a big difference:

  • A cat on wet food gets a lot of its water from meals. It may drink very little from the bowl, and that can be normal.
  • A cat on dry food gets almost no water from meals, so it needs to drink most of its water from the bowl or fountain.
  • A cat on both is somewhere in the middle.

So if your cat eats mostly wet food and you rarely see it drink, that alone is not always a worry. But if your cat eats dry food and hardly drinks, it is worth paying closer attention.

How to tell how much your cat drinks

It is hard to see, because cats take small sips throughout the day. A simple way to check:

  1. Measure the water you put in the bowl in the morning with a measuring cup.
  2. Measure what is left 24 hours later.
  3. The difference is roughly how much your cat drank (a little is lost to evaporation, and other pets will change the number).

Do this for a few days to learn your cat's normal. Then you will notice if it changes.

Why is my cat not drinking water?

Cats come from desert ancestors and often have a low thirst drive. Many simply do not drink much. But there are common, fixable reasons a cat may avoid its water:

  • The water is not fresh. Water that sits all day can collect dust, hair, and food bits. Many cats skip it.
  • The bowl is dirty. A slimy film (biofilm) builds up on bowls and fountains. Wash them often.
  • The bowl is the wrong shape. Some cats do not like their whiskers touching the sides of a narrow, deep bowl. A wide, shallow dish may help.
  • The water is next to the food or litter box. Many cats seem to prefer water a few feet away from food and far from the litter box.
  • They prefer moving water. Some cats would rather drink from the faucet. Read why cats like running water.
  • Stress or change. A move, a new pet, or a loud spot near the bowl can put a cat off drinking.
  • They eat lots of wet food. As above, they may just need less from the bowl.

If your cat suddenly drinks much less than usual, stops eating, or seems unwell, do not wait. Call your vet. And if your cat suddenly drinks much more than usual, that is also worth a vet visit, because increased thirst can be a sign of some health problems.

Cat dehydration signs to watch for

Dehydration means your cat's body has lost more water than it has taken in. Some cat dehydration signs you can check at home:

  • Low energy or hiding more than usual.
  • Dry or sticky gums. Healthy gums are usually moist and slippery.
  • Sunken or dull-looking eyes.
  • Skin tenting: gently lift the skin between the shoulder blades and let go. In a well-hydrated cat it falls back quickly. If it stays up or falls back slowly, your cat may be dehydrated. (This test is less reliable in older or very thin cats.)
  • Less urine in the litter box, or smaller clumps than normal.
  • Not eating, vomiting, or diarrhea, which can cause and worsen dehydration.
  • Constipation or hard, dry stool.

When to call the vet: dehydration can become serious quickly in cats. If you see these signs, especially with vomiting, diarrhea, not eating, or weakness, contact your veterinarian right away. Do not try to force water into your cat's mouth.

Simple tips to help your cat drink more water

  1. Give fresh water every day. Empty, rinse, and refill bowls at least once a day.
  2. Keep bowls and fountains clean. Wash bowls often and clean fountains on a regular schedule. See how often to clean a cat water fountain.
  3. Add more water spots. Put water in a few quiet places around the home, especially in multi-cat homes.
  4. Move water away from food. A few feet apart is enough for many cats.
  5. Try a wide, shallow bowl made of stainless steel, ceramic, or glass.
  6. Add wet food to your cat's diet, if your vet agrees. It is an easy way to add water.
  7. Try a water fountain. Many cats seem to like moving water, and a filter helps keep it cleaner.

Can a water fountain help a cat drink more?

A fountain keeps water moving and runs it through a filter. Many owners say their cats drink more often from a fountain than from a still bowl, though every cat is different. A fountain also holds more water, so the supply does not run out during the day.

Water, food, and routine work together

Drinking is tied to eating. Cats often drink around mealtimes, so steady meal times can mean steadier drinking too. If you feed dry food, keep a fountain or bowl close to the feeding area (but not right next to the food). To find the right food amount for your cat, see how much should a cat eat.

FAQ

How much water should a 10 lb cat drink?

As a general guideline, about 7 to 9 oz a day in total, or roughly one cup. That includes water from food. A cat on wet food may drink much less from the bowl. Ask your vet what is normal for your cat.

Is it normal for a cat not to drink water?

Cats on mostly wet food may drink very little, and that can be normal. But a cat on dry food that rarely drinks, or any cat that suddenly drinks much less, should be checked by a vet.

How long can a cat go without water?

Not long. Cats can become dehydrated within a day or so, and faster in heat or if they are sick. If your cat has not had water, is not eating, or seems weak, call your vet right away.

Why is my cat drinking so much water?

Hot weather, more play, or a switch to dry food can increase thirst. But a big, lasting increase in drinking can be a sign of health problems, so it is worth talking to your vet.

How can I tell if my cat is dehydrated?

Look for low energy, dry or sticky gums, sunken eyes, skin that is slow to fall back when gently lifted, and less urine in the litter box. If you see these signs, contact your vet.

Does wet food count as water for cats?

Yes. Wet food is mostly water, so it adds a lot to your cat's daily intake. Dry food adds very little.

Do cats drink more from a fountain?

Many owners say their cats drink more often from a fountain, but not every cat. What matters most is clean, fresh water. A fountain with a filter, cleaned on schedule, is one easy way to offer that.

Should I put ice cubes in my cat's water?

Some cats enjoy cool water, especially in summer, and a few ice cubes are fine for most cats. If your cat avoids it, keep the water at room temperature.

This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If your pet seems unwell, talk to your veterinarian.
