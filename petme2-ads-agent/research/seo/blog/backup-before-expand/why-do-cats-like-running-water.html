Why do cats like running water?

There is no single proven answer, but these are the explanations you will hear most often from cat owners and pet experts.

1. Instinct

A popular theory is that cats have a natural preference for moving water. In the wild, a flowing stream may have seemed like a better choice than a still puddle. Whether or not that is the full story, many house cats still seem drawn to water that moves.

2. Movement and sound get their attention

Cats are hunters, and they notice movement. A trickle of water, a small splash, or the sparkle of light on moving water can make a cat curious. The gentle sound can also help a cat find the water.

3. It seems fresher

Water in a bowl can collect dust, hair, and food bits. It can also take on the temperature of the room. Moving water often seems cooler and cleaner, and many cats appear to prefer it.

4. Whisker comfort

Some owners notice their cat does not like dipping its face into a deep, narrow bowl. Fountains with a wide, shallow drinking area or a gentle stream let a cat drink without pushing its whiskers against the sides.

5. It is fun

Some cats just like to play. Pawing at a stream of water is entertainment, and a drink comes with it.

Do all cats prefer running water?

No. Every cat is different. Some cats love a fountain from the first day, some take a week or two to get used to it, and some will always prefer a plain bowl. That is why many owners keep a regular water bowl out at first, then watch which one their cat uses more.

Are cat water fountains worth it?

For many households, yes. Here is when a fountain makes the most sense.

A fountain is a good idea if:

  • Your cat drinks from the tap or waits for you to turn on the faucet.
  • Your cat paws at its bowl or tips water out.
  • Your cat often ignores water that has been sitting for a while.
  • You are away for long days and want water that keeps moving and passing through a filter.
  • You have more than one pet and want a larger water supply.

A plain bowl may be fine if:

  • Your cat is happy with its bowl and you can refresh it a few times a day.
  • You do not want to clean a pump and replace filters.
  • Your cat is scared of the sound or movement, even after a slow introduction.

The main trade-off is upkeep. A fountain needs a weekly clean and a filter change every few weeks. Our cleaning guide and filter guide make this simple.

How to help your cat use a new fountain

  1. Place it where your cat already spends time, but a few feet away from the food bowl and far from the litter box.
  2. Keep the old bowl out for the first week or two so your cat always has water it trusts.
  3. Start quietly. If your fountain has flow settings, start low. A quiet pump helps nervous cats.
  4. Let your cat explore. Do not force it. Curiosity usually wins.
  5. Keep it clean. A fountain that smells off or has slime is less inviting.

FAQ

Why does my cat drink from the faucet?

Many cats seem to like moving water. It catches their attention, and it may seem fresher and cooler than water in a bowl. A fountain can give them that moving water all day.

Will my cat drink more with a fountain?

Many owners report that their cats drink more often from a fountain, but every cat is different. If you have questions about how much your cat drinks, talk to your veterinarian.

Why is my cat scared of the water fountain?

New sounds and movement can be scary at first. Keep the old bowl nearby, place the fountain in a calm spot, and give your cat time. A quieter pump also helps.

Can I put a cat fountain next to the food bowl?

It is better to keep them a few feet apart. This keeps food crumbs out of the water and is something many cats seem to prefer.
