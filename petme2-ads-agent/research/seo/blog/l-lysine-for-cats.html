Quick summary

  • What it is: an essential amino acid that cats get from protein in their food.
  • Why owners use it: to support cats with eye and nose flare-ups, often linked to feline herpesvirus (FHV-1).
  • What the research says: mixed. It is not a proven treatment, and it does not cure herpes.
  • Safety: generally considered well tolerated. Mild stomach upset is possible.
  • Forms: powders, gels, treats and soft chews.
  • Always: get a vet check for eye problems, and ask your vet before adding any supplement.

What is L-lysine?

L-lysine (often just called lysine) is an amino acid. Amino acids are the small building blocks that make up protein. Lysine is called an "essential" amino acid, which means the body cannot make it on its own and has to get it from food.

A complete, balanced cat food already contains lysine, because meat and other proteins are rich in it. So when people talk about L-lysine for cats, they usually mean extra lysine given as a supplement, on top of a normal diet.

Why do cat owners give L-lysine?

Most owners use L-lysine because of feline herpesvirus type 1 (FHV-1). This is a very common virus in cats and a major cause of "cat flu" or upper respiratory signs. It is not the same as human herpes, and it does not spread to people.

Many cats catch FHV-1 as kittens. After the first illness, the virus often stays in the body in a quiet (latent) state. Later, it can "wake up" and cause flare-ups, often during times of stress, like moving house, boarding, a new pet, or illness.

Common signs owners notice during a flare-up

  • Sneezing
  • Watery or goopy eyes
  • Squinting or red eyes
  • A runny or stuffy nose
  • Eating less, because a cat that cannot smell its food may lose interest

These signs can also come from other infections, allergies, dental problems and more. That is why a vet visit is important. Eye problems in particular can get worse quickly and should be checked.

How is L-lysine supposed to help?

The theory goes like this. The herpesvirus needs another amino acid, arginine, to copy itself. Lysine and arginine are similar in shape. The idea is that if there is more lysine around, it may compete with arginine and make it harder for the virus to multiply.

This idea came mostly from lab studies. It is a reasonable theory, but a theory that works in a lab dish does not always work the same way in a living cat.

One important point: cats need arginine. It is an essential nutrient for them. So the goal is never to remove arginine from your cat's diet. Lysine supplements are meant to be added to a normal, complete cat food.

Does L-lysine work for cats? What the research says

The honest answer is that nobody knows for sure, because the research on L-lysine for cats is mixed.

  • Some earlier studies were encouraging. In some small studies, cats given lysine seemed to have milder eye signs or less virus shedding.
  • Later studies and reviews were less positive. Some larger studies, including studies in shelter cats, did not find a clear benefit. Reviews of the research have concluded that there is not strong evidence that lysine prevents or treats FHV-1 infection.
  • Vets have different views. Some vets still suggest lysine for certain cats as a low-risk option. Others no longer recommend it. Your own vet knows your cat and can help you decide.

What this means for you:

  • L-lysine is not a cure for feline herpes. Nothing currently removes the virus from the body.
  • It is not a replacement for vet care or for medicines your vet prescribes.
  • Some owners feel it is a helpful part of their cat's routine. That is a personal choice to make together with your vet.

Is L-lysine safe for cats?

L-lysine is generally considered well tolerated in cats when given as directed. Things to keep in mind:

  • Mild stomach upset, like soft stool or vomiting, can happen. Stop and call your vet if it does.
  • Use a product made for cats. Human lysine tablets may contain other ingredients, flavors or amounts that are not meant for cats. Do not use them unless your vet says so.
  • Follow the label and your vet. More is not better.
  • Tell your vet about every supplement your cat takes, especially if your cat has kidney disease, other health problems, or takes medicine.
  • Pregnant or nursing cats, and young kittens: ask your vet first.

How do you give L-lysine to a cat?

L-lysine comes in a few forms. The best one is the one your cat will actually take.

Tips for success

  • Be consistent. Give it at the same time each day, for example with breakfast.
  • Start small with a new texture or flavor so your cat can get used to it.
  • Do not hide it in a whole meal your cat may not finish. Mix powder into a small portion first.
  • Keep a simple diary of sneezing, eye discharge and appetite, so you and your vet can see if anything changes.

How else can you support a cat prone to flare-ups?

Whatever you decide about lysine, these everyday habits are good for any cat, and especially for cats that tend to get sneezy when stressed:

  • Keep stress low. Stress is often linked to flare-ups. Keep routines steady, give hiding spots and high perches, and introduce changes slowly.
  • Keep meals regular. A cat with a stuffy nose may eat less. Warming wet food slightly can make it smell stronger and more tempting. An automatic feeder can keep dry-food meal times steady when you are busy.
  • Encourage drinking. Fresh, clean water matters even more when your cat is under the weather. Many cats drink more readily from moving water, which is why owners use a cat water fountain. Read why cats like running water.
  • Keep things clean. Wash bowls and fountains often. Our guide on how often to clean a cat water fountain keeps it simple.
  • Stay up to date on vaccines and regular check-ups. Ask your vet what your cat needs.

When should you see your vet?

Do not wait it out with supplements if you notice:

  • Squinting, a cloudy eye, or an eye your cat keeps closed
  • Thick yellow or green discharge from the eyes or nose
  • Not eating for more than a day
  • Breathing with an open mouth, or noisy or hard breathing
  • Tiredness, hiding, or fever
  • Symptoms in a kitten, a senior cat, or a cat with other health problems

Frequently asked questions

What does L-lysine do for cats?

L-lysine is an amino acid that cats get from protein in their food. As a supplement, owners often give it to support cats prone to eye and nose flare-ups linked to feline herpesvirus. The research on whether it helps is mixed, so talk to your vet before you start.

Does L-lysine work for cats?

The evidence is mixed. Some early, small studies looked encouraging, but later studies and reviews have not shown a clear benefit. Some vets still suggest it as a low-risk option for certain cats and others do not, so ask your own vet.

Does L-lysine cure feline herpes?

No, L-lysine does not cure feline herpes. There is no treatment that removes feline herpesvirus from the body, and L-lysine is not a treatment for it. Your vet can explain the options if your cat has flare-ups.

What is feline herpesvirus?

Feline herpesvirus type 1 (FHV-1) is a very common cat virus and a major cause of upper respiratory signs, sometimes called cat flu. Many cats catch it as kittens, and the virus can stay quiet in the body and flare up later, often during stress. Only a vet can confirm what is causing your cat's symptoms.

Can humans catch feline herpes from cats?

No, feline herpesvirus does not spread to people. It is a different virus from human herpes and only affects cats. It can spread between cats, so ask your vet about care in multi-cat homes.

What are the signs of a feline herpes flare-up?

Common signs owners notice include sneezing, watery or goopy eyes, squinting, red eyes, a runny or stuffy nose and eating less. These signs can also come from other infections, allergies or dental problems. Eye problems can get worse quickly, so see your vet.

Is L-lysine safe for cats?

L-lysine is generally considered well tolerated in cats when given as directed. Mild stomach upset, like soft stool or vomiting, is possible. Use a product made for cats and check with your vet first, especially if your cat has health problems or takes medicine.

Can I give my cat human L-lysine?

It is best not to give your cat human L-lysine unless your vet says so. Human products may contain other ingredients, flavors or amounts that are not meant for cats. Choose a product made for cats and follow the label.

Can kittens have L-lysine?

Ask your vet before giving L-lysine to a kitten. Kittens are small and still growing, and your vet can tell you whether a supplement makes sense. Kittens with eye or nose symptoms should always see a vet.

How long does L-lysine take to work?

There is no clear answer, because research has not shown a reliable benefit or timeline. If you and your vet decide to try it, give it consistently and keep a simple diary of sneezing, eye discharge and appetite. Check back with your vet to decide whether to continue.

Can L-lysine help with cat sneezing?

Some owners give L-lysine to cats that sneeze during herpes flare-ups, but evidence that it helps is not strong. Sneezing has many causes, from infections to dental problems. See your vet if sneezing is frequent or comes with other signs.

What forms of L-lysine are there for cats?

L-lysine for cats comes as powders, gels or pastes, treats and soft chews. Powder mixes into wet food, gels can be licked off a finger or paw, and chews are given like a treat. The best form is the one your cat will take every day.

Can L-lysine replace vet treatment?

No, L-lysine is not a replacement for vet care or for any medicine your vet prescribes. Squinting, cloudy eyes, thick yellow or green discharge, not eating for more than a day or hard breathing all need a vet. A supplement can only ever be an add-on your vet agrees with.

Does my cat need L-lysine if it eats a complete cat food?

No, a healthy cat does not need extra L-lysine, because a complete, balanced cat food already provides the lysine it needs. Extra lysine is an optional supplement, not a requirement. Ask your vet if it is right for your cat.
