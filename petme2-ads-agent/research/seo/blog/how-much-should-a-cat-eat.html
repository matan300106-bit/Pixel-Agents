Quick answer: how much should a cat eat? As a general guideline, a healthy indoor adult cat of about 10 lb needs roughly 220 to 260 calories a day. For most dry foods, that is about 1/2 to 2/3 cup a day, split into 2 or more meals. The right amount for your cat depends on the food, age, activity, and body shape, so always check the feeding guide on the food bag and ask your vet.

How much should a cat eat a day?

There is no single number that fits every cat. A small, older, sleepy cat needs much less than a young, active cat. And two foods that look the same can have very different calories.

That is why vets usually talk about calories, not cups. Once you know about how many calories your cat needs, you can turn it into cups or cans using the label on your cat's food.

For a healthy, spayed or neutered indoor adult cat, a common general guideline is about 20 to 26 calories per pound of body weight per day. Very active cats may need more. Cats that need to lose weight usually need less, and your vet should set that number.

Cat feeding chart by weight

This chart is a general guideline for healthy indoor adult cats at a healthy weight. It is a starting point, not a rule. Your food's feeding guide and your vet's advice come first.

Cat weight Calories per day (about) Dry food per day* Wet food per day**
5 lb 130 to 160 about 1/3 cup about 1½ to 2 cans
8 lb 185 to 220 about 1/2 cup about 2½ cans
10 lb 220 to 260 about 1/2 to 2/3 cup about 3 cans
12 lb 250 to 300 about 2/3 to 3/4 cup about 3 to 3½ cans
15 lb 295 to 355 about 3/4 to 7/8 cup about 3½ to 4½ cans

*Based on a dry food with about 400 calories per 8 oz cup. Many dry foods fall somewhere around 350 to 450, so check yours. **Based on 3 oz cans with about 80 calories each. Wet food calories vary a lot, so check the label. If you feed both dry and wet, split the daily calories between them.

Important: if your cat is overweight, do not feed by its current weight. A 15 lb cat that should weigh 11 lb needs a plan based on its ideal weight, and weight loss should be slow. Ask your vet for a safe target.

How to use your food label

  1. Find the calories on the bag or can. It is often written as "kcal/cup" or "kcal/can".
  2. Find the feeding guide on the same label. It shows a range by weight.
  3. Start at the lower or middle part of that range for an indoor cat.
  4. Measure with a real measuring cup or a kitchen scale, not a coffee mug.
  5. Remember treats. Many vets suggest keeping treats to about 10% of daily calories.

How often to feed a cat

How often to feed a cat depends on age and your routine. Here is a simple general guide:

  • Kittens (under about 6 months): 3 to 4 small meals a day. Their stomachs are small and they are growing fast. Use a kitten food and follow its label.
  • Older kittens (about 6 to 12 months): usually 2 to 3 meals a day.
  • Adult cats (1 to about 10 years): at least 2 meals a day, about 8 to 12 hours apart. Many cats like 3 to 4 smaller meals even more.
  • Senior cats: often do well with small, frequent meals. Older cats can lose or gain weight more easily, so regular vet checks matter.

In nature, cats eat many small meals across the day and night. That is why some cats beg loudly at 5 a.m. Splitting the same daily amount into more meals can help a cat feel less hungry between meals, without adding calories.

Free feeding vs scheduled meals

Free feeding means leaving a full bowl out all day. It is easy, but many indoor cats eat more than they need this way. It also makes it hard to notice if your cat stops eating, which can be an early sign of a problem.

Scheduled meals mean you serve a measured amount at set times. This makes it easier to control portions, see how much your cat eats, and spot changes early. For most indoor cats, scheduled meals are the easier way to keep a healthy weight.

A simple cat feeding schedule

A cat feeding schedule does not need to be complicated. Pick times that fit your day, and keep them the same, including weekends. Cats love routine.

Meals per day Example times Good for
2 meals 7 a.m. and 7 p.m. Most adult cats, simple routines
3 meals 7 a.m., 1 p.m., 9 p.m. Older kittens, cats that beg between meals
4 meals 6 a.m., 11 a.m., 4 p.m., 10 p.m. Young kittens, fast eaters, cats on a weight plan
4+ small meals Add a small meal at 4 a.m. Cats that wake you up early for food

The key rule: divide your cat's daily amount by the number of meals. More meals should not mean more food. If your cat needs about 1/2 cup a day and eats 4 meals, each meal is about 1/8 cup.

Signs you may be feeding too much or too little

The scale and your hands tell you more than the chart. Check your cat's body every few weeks:

  • Healthy weight: you can feel the ribs easily under a thin layer of fat, and you can see a waist from above.
  • Too much food: ribs are hard to feel, there is no waist, or the belly sags and swings. Slowly cut back a little and talk to your vet.
  • Too little food: ribs, spine, or hip bones are easy to see, or your cat is losing weight. Talk to your vet.

Also call your vet if your cat suddenly eats much less, stops eating, or seems very hungry all the time. Cats should not go without food for long, and changes in appetite can be a sign of a health issue.

Tips for feeding your cat the right amount

  • Weigh your cat once a month. Step on a scale holding your cat, then without, and subtract.
  • Change food slowly. Mix new food with old over about 7 to 10 days to avoid upset stomachs.
  • Re-check the amount when you switch food. The new food may have more or fewer calories per cup.
  • Count treats and toppers. Small extras add up fast.
  • Keep fresh water close by, but a few feet away from the food. Cats on dry food get very little water from their meals. Learn more in how much water a cat should drink.

FAQ

How much should a cat eat a day in cups?

For many indoor adult cats of about 10 lb, it is roughly 1/2 to 2/3 cup of dry food a day, as a general guideline. But calories per cup vary a lot between foods, so check your food's label and ask your vet.

How much wet food should a cat eat a day?

As a rough guide, a 10 lb adult cat might eat about three 3 oz cans a day if wet food is its only food. Wet food calories vary, so use the label on your can. If you also feed dry food, reduce the wet food to keep the daily total the same.

How often should I feed my cat?

Most adult cats should eat at least 2 meals a day, about 8 to 12 hours apart. Kittens need 3 to 4 meals. Many adult cats are happier with 3 or 4 smaller meals made from the same daily amount.

Is it OK to feed a cat once a day?

Most vets suggest at least two meals a day for adult cats. One big meal can leave a cat very hungry for a long time. If you have a reason to feed once a day, ask your vet first.

Why is my cat always hungry?

It can be habit, boredom, too-long gaps between meals, or a food that does not fill your cat up. Splitting the same amount into more meals often helps. But a big increase in appetite can also be a sign of a health problem, so talk to your vet if it is new.

How much should a kitten eat?

Kittens need more calories per pound than adults and should eat 3 to 4 small meals a day. Use a food made for kittens and follow its feeding guide. Your vet can help you adjust as your kitten grows.

Should I free feed my cat?

Free feeding is easy but can lead to weight gain in many indoor cats, and it makes it harder to notice if your cat stops eating. Scheduled, measured meals are usually easier to manage. An automatic feeder can serve those meals for you.

This article is general information, not veterinary advice. If your pet seems unwell, talk to your veterinarian.
