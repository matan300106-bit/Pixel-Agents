Quick answer: clean your cat water fountain about once a week, and top it up with fresh water every day or two. If you have more than one cat, a hot room, or you see slime or hair building up, clean it more often (every 3 to 5 days). A full clean takes about 10 to 15 minutes once you know the steps below.

Quick summary

  • Daily or every other day: top up with fresh water and check the water level.
  • Weekly: empty, wash the bowl, cover, and filter housing; rinse the pump.
  • Every 2 to 4 weeks: replace the filter (or as the maker suggests).
  • Monthly: take the pump apart and clean the small impeller.
  • Use: warm water, mild unscented dish soap, and a small brush. Rinse well.

How often should you clean a cat water fountain?

Most fountain makers suggest a full clean about once a week. That is a good baseline for one cat in a normal home. Several things can mean you need to clean more often:

  • More pets. Two or three cats (or a cat and a dog) means more drinking, more hair, and more food bits in the water.
  • Warm weather. Water evaporates faster and residue builds up faster in a warm room.
  • Hard water. White mineral spots on the pump and bowl are a sign to descale more often.
  • Location. A fountain near the food bowl or litter box tends to collect more debris.

A simple rule: if the water looks cloudy, the surface feels slimy, or you can see hair in the bowl, it is time to clean, whatever the calendar says.

What you need

  • Warm water
  • Mild, unscented dish soap
  • A soft sponge or cloth
  • A small bottle brush or old toothbrush
  • A cotton swab or pipe cleaner for the pump and tubes
  • White vinegar (optional, for mineral buildup)

Avoid bleach, strong cleaners, and scented soaps. Cats have a strong sense of smell, and leftover scent can put them off drinking from the fountain.

How to clean a cat water fountain, step by step

1. Unplug it first

Always unplug the fountain before you touch the water or the pump. Safety first.

2. Empty the water and take it apart

Pour out the old water. Remove the top cover, the filter, and the pump. Most fountains come apart by hand without tools. Keep the small parts together so nothing gets lost.

3. Wash the bowl and cover

Wash the bowl and cover with warm, soapy water. Use the brush for corners and the spout where slime likes to hide. Some fountain parts are dishwasher safe (top rack), but check your manual first. Stainless steel parts, like the bowl on the PETME2 3.2L stainless steel fountain, are usually the easiest to scrub clean.

4. Clean the pump

The pump is the part most people forget. Hair and residue can build up inside it and make it louder or weaker over time.

  • Open the pump cover (it usually twists or pops off).
  • Gently remove the small impeller (the little spinning part).
  • Rinse it and use a cotton swab or small brush to clean inside the pump housing.
  • Put it back together and make sure it spins freely.

Doing this about once a month (or every week in multi-pet homes) helps keep the pump quiet and the flow steady.

5. Check or replace the filter

Rinse the filter under clean water (no soap) if it is still in its replacement window. If it is old, discolored, or the flow is slower, replace it. See our full cat water fountain filter guide for timing and tips.

6. Descale if needed

If you see white or chalky spots, soak the affected parts in a mix of half white vinegar and half water for 15 to 30 minutes. Then scrub and rinse very well until there is no vinegar smell left.

7. Rinse, rebuild, refill

Rinse every part with clean water so no soap is left. Put the pump back, then the filter, then the cover. Fill with fresh water to the max line, plug it in, and check that water flows normally.

How to clean a stainless steel cat water fountain

The routine above works for every material, but stainless steel has a few extra tips:

  • Use a soft sponge or cloth. Skip steel wool and gritty powders. They can scratch the smooth finish that makes steel so easy to clean.
  • Wipe off water spots with diluted vinegar, then rinse with clean water.
  • Dry the steel parts with a towel before you put the fountain back together. This helps prevent new water spots.
  • Check the manual before using the dishwasher. The PETME2 3.2L stainless steel fountain has dishwasher-safe detachable parts, but the pump and power cable must always stay out.

Tips to keep your fountain cleaner for longer

  • Move it away from food. A few feet from the food bowl keeps kibble crumbs out of the water.
  • Use filtered or bottled water if your tap water is very hard. This slows mineral buildup.
  • Brush your cat. Less loose hair means less hair in the pump.
  • Pick a fountain that is easy to take apart. Fewer parts and a wide bowl make weekly cleaning much faster.
  • Keep a spare filter at home so you never have to skip a change.
  • Set a phone reminder for the same day each week, like "Sunday fountain clean."

Signs your fountain needs cleaning now

  • Cloudy water or a film on the surface
  • Pink or brown slime on the bowl or spout
  • The pump sounds louder or makes a buzzing noise
  • Water flow is weaker than usual
  • Your cat seems less interested in drinking from it

Troubleshooting: loud, weak, or not working

Why is my cat water fountain so loud?

The most common causes are simple to fix:

  • Low water. When the level drops, the pump can pull in air and start to hum or gurgle. Top it up.
  • Hair in the impeller. Take the pump apart and clean it (see step 4 above).
  • Trapped air after a refill. Let it run for a few minutes. Most bubbles clear on their own.
  • The pump is not sitting flat. Press it down so it sits level on the bottom of the bowl.

Why did my fountain stop working?

Check the plug and adapter first. Then check the water level. Some fountains, like the Classic 2L, have anti-dry protection that is designed to protect the pump when the water gets too low. After that, look for a clogged filter or an impeller that will not spin. A good clean fixes most problems.

Why does my cat keep knocking over the fountain?

Some cats paw at moving water for fun. Others bump a light fountain while drinking. Try a calmer corner, a flat and stable surface, and a mat underneath. A heavier fountain, such as a stainless steel model, is harder to push around.

Common cleaning mistakes to avoid

  • Only topping up. Adding water without ever emptying the fountain leaves old water at the bottom.
  • Skipping the pump. Hidden hair and slime build up inside it.
  • Using bleach, strong cleaners or scented soap. Leftover smells can put cats off drinking.
  • Washing the filter with soap. Rinse it with plain water only.
  • Getting the plug or power adapter wet. Always unplug first and keep electrical parts dry.
  • Letting the water drop below the pump. This makes the pump noisy and can shorten its life.
  • Not rinsing after vinegar. Rinse until there is no vinegar smell left.

FAQ

How often should I change the water in a cat fountain?

Top up the water every day or two, and fully replace it during your weekly clean. If the water ever looks dirty, change it right away.

Can I put my cat water fountain in the dishwasher?

Some parts can go on the top rack, but never put the pump or any electrical parts in the dishwasher. Always check your product manual first.

Is vinegar safe for cleaning a cat fountain?

Diluted white vinegar is commonly used to remove mineral buildup. The key is to rinse very well afterward so no taste or smell is left in the fountain.

Why is there pink slime in my cat fountain?

Pink or orange slime is a common sign of residue and bacteria buildup in standing or slow-moving wet areas. It usually means the fountain is due for a full clean. Scrub all parts, including the pump and spout.

Do I need to clean the fountain if I use a filter?

Yes. A filter helps catch hair and debris, but it does not replace regular cleaning of the bowl and pump.

How do I clean a cat water fountain pump?

Unplug the fountain and lift out the pump. Open the pump cover, slide out the small impeller, and rinse both. Use a cotton swab or small brush inside the pump housing, then put it back together and check that the impeller spins freely. Do this about once a month, or weekly if several pets share the fountain.

Why is my cat water fountain so loud?

Usually it is low water, hair in the impeller, or air trapped in the pump. Top up the water, clean the pump, and make sure the pump sits flat on the bottom of the bowl.

How often should you replace a cat water fountain?

There is no fixed date. With regular cleaning and filter changes, a fountain can last a long time. Think about replacing it (or the worn part) if the pump keeps failing even after cleaning, the bowl is cracked or chipped, or plastic parts are so scratched they no longer come clean.
