Know your creosote

Not all soot is the same

Creosote hardens the longer it's left alone. The earlier we catch it, the faster and cheaper it comes out.

Stage 1

Flaky soot

Normal

Dry, dusty and black. A standard sweep brushes it out in one visit. This is where an annual cleaning keeps you.

Stage 2

Crunchy tar

Watch it

Shiny black flakes, like puffed cereal. Comes from smoldering fires and wet wood. Needs rotary tools to break it up.

Stage 3

Glazed creosote

Fire risk

A hard, glassy coat that drips and pools. Highly flammable. Removed with chemical treatment and specialty rotary heads.

Your visit

What happens when we show up

Most sweeps take about an hour. You don't need to move furniture or be on the roof.

  1. Cover up

    Drop cloths go down from the door to the hearth. The firebox gets sealed and a HEPA vacuum runs the whole time.

  2. Sweep

    Rotary brushes work up through the damper and smoke shelf to the top of the flue, then we vacuum the debris.

  3. Inspect

    A camera runs the full length of the liner looking for cracks, gaps and leftover glaze.

  4. Report

    You get photos, the footage and a plain-language write-up. Any repair comes as a written estimate, never a surprise.
